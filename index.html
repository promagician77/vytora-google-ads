The six, and what actually changed

On 28 October 2026 HHS announced limited enforcement discretion for compounding six peptide bulk substances. That changed the supply side. It did not move any of the three policy lines that decide whether you can advertise them.

What HHS did

  • Enforcement discretion for compounding, under defined conditions
  • Explicitly not an FDA approval, and it does not establish clinical safety or effectiveness
  • The six were not added to the 503A bulks list. That rulemaking is still open

Why this matters twice over

An FDA warning letter is its own problem. It is also a Google problem, because the policy restricts "Products that have been subject to any government or regulatory action or warning". A letter in the category raises the compliance floor for everyone advertising in it.

The two facts that force this design

  • Google does not sign a BAA for Google Ads. There is no setting that makes it a business associate, so the constraint is absolute. Protected health information never reaches Google, through a tag, a conversion name, an audience or a parameter.
  • AHA v. Becerra narrowed what counts. On 20 June 2024 a federal court vacated the part of OCR's tracking bulletin covering IP addresses on unauthenticated public pages, and HHS withdrew its appeal that August. That ruling is why the public half of this funnel is workable. It is not a general permission.

The approach I would not take

Cross-domain linking, letting the Google tag follow the user onto the intake subdomain, is the obvious move and the wrong one. It puts a Google tag on identifying pages and makes Google a recipient of data it must not hold. Carrying the click ID as an order field reaches the same destination without that.

No name, no email, no date of birth, no treatment, no intake answers, no page path that implies a condition. The payload is built by allowlist, never by deleting fields, because a blocklist fails open the day Bask adds a column.

Conversion names are a disclosure too

Action names appear in the interface, in shared reports and in exports. A conversion called weight_loss_rx_complete tells a story to everyone who sees the account.

Identical across every treatment line. The treatment split lives in your reporting, never in the ad platform.

Duplicate conversions, which your brief names

  • Two routes reporting one order. Avoided by having one route. The server is the only thing that reports a purchase.
  • Retries and replays. The uploader is idempotent on order ID. Google deduplicates on identical order ID within a conversion action, and our own cache refuses a second send regardless, because relying on one guard is relying on none.
  • Renewals counted as new sales. Separate action, excluded from campaign return on ad spend by default.

Eighteen validation tests cover this end to end, from "does the GCLID survive the subdomain hop" to "does a replayed webhook double count". T02 is the one that fails most often in this architecture, and it fails silently.

The negative list is a policy control, not just a budget one

132 terms across ten groups at launch. The group that matters most is the first: no prescription, without a doctor, research chemical, not for human consumption. An ad serving against those is reviewed in that context, and context is what an editorial reviewer sees.

Two groups people forget. Veterinary, because BPC-157 and TB-500 carry heavy animal-health search volume. And dosage and self-administration, which is high volume, near zero conversion, and a poor context for a prescriber's ad.

What matters technically is narrow and I would ask it on day one: does Bask fire a webhook on completed purchase, can an arbitrary custom field ride along on an order, and does the payload carry patient fields that the server must strip. 04How would you validate tracking from ad click through completed purchase?

Eighteen named tests, fourteen of them before launch. Click a live ad on a test budget, confirm the GCLID in the cookie, complete a test intake, confirm the same GCLID on the Bask order, confirm no Google tag fired anywhere on intake or the portal, place a test purchase, confirm one conversion with the right value against the right action, then fire the same webhook three times and confirm it stays one.

This week. Three weeks to launch: determination and tracking architecture in week 1, tracking built and validated in week 2, campaigns built and phase 1 live in week 3. Phase 1 can go live earlier if Bask answers quickly, because it gates on tracking alone.
